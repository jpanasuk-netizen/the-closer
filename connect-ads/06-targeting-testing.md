# Targeting, budget notes, and the test checklist

**Status:** draft only. No ad account work and no spend until Jeremy approves a platform, a budget, and a destination URL.

This section does **not** include a predicted CPA, ROAS, or lead volume. Kai Bax’s advertised call counts, “$0 down,” and “hidden audience” are his commercial claims. The hidden audience is not described in any free source found on Nov 16, 2026. Do not guess it.

## What we actually know from public material

- His acquisition channel is Meta (Facebook / Instagram). Ad-library scrapes in 2026 showed a large share of video (about 83%), some image, a little carousel, with reach described as mostly United States, traffic to scaleclients.io.
- He says they use one audience for every ad, and markets that audience as hidden. It is not public. Treat “broad / Advantage+ / interest plus creative testing” as the honest starting point, not a secret he didn’t publish.
- The transferable media-buying idea, from the ScaleClients homepage: send **qualified** conversion events back to Meta (booked-and-fit, or an actual purchase), not raw clicks, so the algorithm hunts buyers.
- His sales page also says the same funnel can be put in front of people on organic channels if there is no ad budget. That part is usable immediately: LinkedIn, the live page, and the house hub.

## Recommended starting point for CONNECT

**Offer economics first.** $99 once cannot support a high-ticket appointment engine. Until a gated test produces a real cost per email or cost per $99 wire, keep paid spend small and treat it as a creative test, not a growth engine. Organic can use the same scripts without a media budget.

**Geo:** start where Jeremy can actually support people — English-speaking, and be ready to name New Auburn, WI as the business location. Do not claim a local-service radius CONNECT doesn’t have. If a platform requires a country, prefer the United States first, then expand only after a real result.

**Who to aim at (interests / custom lists, not a promise they’ll convert):**

- People already in the market for AI assistants: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Copilot, Notion, productivity tools
- Solopreneurs, operators, consultants, and small-team founders who draft a lot of email or content
- Warm lists only if Jeremy already has permission: existing newsletter, LinkedIn connections, prior customers. Do not scrape.

**Who to exclude, if the platform lets you:** job seekers for “media buyer” roles (his ads attract those comments), people looking for “guaranteed leads,” and any audience built on his brand terms. We are not bidding to steal his sales-call buyers.

**Placement:** start with Reels / Shorts / in-feed video, because the scripts are built for muted autoplay. Add static image variants of the same hook, not a new offer. Turn off placements you cannot actually design for (a 200-character description with no first-frame text).

**Events to fire, if ads ever run:**

1. View of the quiz or CONNECT page (for debugging only)
2. Lead: completed quiz or one-pager request, with a real email
3. Purchase or “started wire”: only when $99 is actually collected, or a written yes that Jeremy treats as an order

Do not optimize the campaign for the first event. Optimizing for views will spend the budget on tourists.

**Creative testing, not audience trivia:**

- Hold the offer and the destination still.
- Test hooks / first-frame lines first (the five headlines in the scripts file).
- Then test body (tab vs desk, the gate, two bills, quiz, skeptic).
- Then test format (talking-head UGC vs desk still vs page-on-laptop).
- Refresh when frequency is clearly fatiguing the hook, not on a made-up 7-day ritual.

**Budget language you can use with Jeremy, without inventing a result:**

- Pick a daily cap he is willing to lose on a test.
- Run long enough to see hook-level differences, not one afternoon.
- Kill a hook that can’t beat the others on the metric you chose (usually cost per qualified email, later cost per paid wire).
- Do not raise spend just because a video looks “busy.”
- Do not scale until the destination, the follow-up, and the fulfillment (the actual wire) can take more people.

If he would rather not spend at all, use the scripts organically on LinkedIn and, if it fits, as YouTube tags/end-screens. The formula still works without a pixel.

## Iteration checklist (weekly, after a test starts)

- [ ] Which first-frame line earned the cheapest *qualified* action? Keep it. Kill the rest or park them.
- [ ] Which body lost people after the hook? (They clicked, then bounced.) Fix mechanism or the two-bills explanation, not the headline.
- [ ] Any comment asking “is this a monthly fee?” — the ad still isn’t clear. Patch the first screen.
- [ ] Any comment asking for a lead guarantee — don’t argue in the thread; that person is not the buyer. The DM reply in the follow-up file already covers this.
- [ ] Proof still honest? If a real customer later gives a usable quote, add it. Don’t pad.
- [ ] Fulfillment still keeps up?
- [ ] NESB: did a new line sneak in that hits none of New / Easy / Safe / Big?
- [ ] Slop pass on any rewrite
- [ ] Still draft-gated: no one raised budget or published a new page without Jeremy

## What “winning” means here

A winning ad is one that produces a paid $99 wire, or at least a qualified email from someone who picked a path, at a cost Jeremy is willing to pay, without lying. Until those numbers exist, there is no winner to report — only drafts and tests.
